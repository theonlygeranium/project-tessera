The readiness report checks a course against a rubric: a list of standards, each made up of items. It opens from a lesson's readiness panel in the editor, and from a course-level readiness page.

## Rubrics and items

Every item is judged one of three ways:

- **Automatic** — computed directly from the course: outcomes are present, every module states an objective, every check, scenario, and assignment is tagged with the outcomes it assesses, the accessibility score meets the policy, reading level, navigation instructions, required template structure, time estimates, and more.
- **AI-assisted** — Tessera judges the item against its written criteria and returns a finding with evidence and a suggested fix. Like every other AI output in Tessera, a finding is a draft: it needs review before it counts. An accepted "likely met" finding counts as met, an accepted "likely not met" finding counts as not met, and anything dismissed or unclear stays "needs review."
- **Attestation** — a named reviewer confirms the item directly, with a note and a date. A reviewer can also mark an automatic item "not applicable" with a note, but can't attest past an automatic result any other way — that one is fixed by fixing the course.

## Built-in rubrics

Tessera ships two rubrics:

- **The Tessera course standard** — written in plain language, in eight areas: overview and navigation, outcomes and objectives, assessment and feedback, materials, engagement and interaction, technology and AI, learner support, and accessibility.

## Outcomes and alignment

Course outcomes are entities with their own ids, not just text. A check, scenario, or assignment can be tagged with the outcomes it assesses, and the automatic alignment items read those tags directly — confirming both that every assessment is tagged and that every outcome is actually assessed somewhere.

## Scoring and publishing

A standard's result is met, partly met, not met, or not applicable (when it has no items that apply to this course). The course's overall percentage is the share of applicable items that are met or attested, rounded down; items marked not applicable are excluded rather than counted against the course.

The rubric result is **advisory by default** — instructors see it, but it doesn't block anything. An administrator can set a minimum result that publishing must meet, the same way the accessibility policy can: the server enforces it when a lesson is published, not only on the screen, and the instructor sees the rubric, the course's percentage, and the minimum it needs.

Automatic items that aren't met link to a fix: the exact block, module, lesson, assignment, or course setting responsible. AI-assisted and attestation items are resolved by reviewing the finding or attesting.
