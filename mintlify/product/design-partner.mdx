**Start from a syllabus** is a second way to begin in Build with AI. Instead of describing a course in a prompt, an instructor uploads the syllabus they already have (or pastes a training brief), and Tessera's AI, labelled **Design partner**, reads it the way an instructional designer would. It says what it understood, asks about what the document doesn't say, proposes ways to structure the course, and shows exactly what it would create before it creates anything.

The instructor stays the subject-matter expert and the instructor of record. The Design partner handles sequencing, alignment, scaffolding, and quality checks, and it shows the evidence behind every suggestion. Learners never see the Design partner label. Kept content shows the usual "Drafted with AI · edited by" line, like any other [AI draft](/product/authoring).

This page uses one fictional example throughout: **STAT 110 · Reasoning with Data**, taught by Dr. Amara Okafor at Meridian State University.

## The six stages

The stepper across the top of the flow reads **Start · Read · Confirm · Approaches · Preview · Review**. You can go back to an earlier stage at any time. Nothing is added to the course until you apply the preview, and even then it all arrives as drafts.

### 1. Start

1. Open Build with AI for the course and choose **Start from a syllabus**.
2. Give it the source in one of three ways:
   - **Upload a file.** PDF or DOCX, up to 25 MB, one syllabus per course. Scanned PDFs are read with OCR first.
   - **Use a file already in the course**, such as last term's version.
   - **Paste text.** A training brief or a competency list works too.
3. Read the scope panel, which says what the Design partner will and won't use:
   - **Only this syllabus.** No rosters, grades, or student work. Any student name found in the document is removed before analysis.
   - **Yours.** The syllabus and everything drafted from it belong to you. Nothing is used to train a model.
   - **Cited.** Every claim points to the page it came from. Readings come only from your syllabus. Where one is missing, you get a placeholder, never an invented citation.
   - **Drafts only.** Nothing is written to the course until you approve a preview, and one action undoes everything that was added.
4. Tick the box to confirm you understand, then choose **Read my syllabus**.

Optionally, tick **Remember my teaching preferences for future courses**. Your teaching approach, voice, and assessment preferences are then saved to your instructor profile, where you can edit or clear them at any time.

<Note>
Word 97–2003 files (`.doc`) can't be read. If you upload one, Tessera tells you so and asks you to save it as `.docx` or PDF first. The same goes for older PowerPoint (`.ppt`) and RTF files.
</Note>

If your administrator has switched the Design partner off for your program, the Start page says so and points to the AI policy.

### 2. Read

While the Design partner reads, you see progress on the Start page. You can leave the page; reading runs in the background and usually takes one to three minutes. When it finishes, you get an **instructional read** that opens with what it understood, for example that STAT 110 is a 3-credit, 15-week introductory course with six outcomes, five graded components, and a weekly schedule.

The read has these parts:

- **Course profile.** Credits, term length, meeting pattern, modality, level, prerequisites, enrolment, and materials. Each field shows where it came from: read from a page, inferred, supplied by you, or missing.
- **Outcomes and how they're assessed.** Your outcomes appear word for word, with the Design partner's reading of each outcome's level and whether anything in the syllabus assesses it. It flags outcomes whose verb can't be observed, and outcomes that are assessed by a task that can't show the verb.
- **Estimated student hours per week**, compared with the weekly budget for the credit hours, with the assumptions listed so you can edit them.
- **Learner-centeredness signals**, with the passages they came from.
- **Gaps**, such as graded components with no stated purpose or criteria.

**Citations.** Every claim carries a citation chip. For PDFs, that's the page, such as "p. 3". For Word files, which have no fixed pages, it's the section heading the passage sits under. Hover or focus a chip to see the passage itself. If a claim is wrong, mark the field as wrong and it becomes a question.

For a training brief, the read uses the brief's seat time in place of the credit-hour budget.

### 3. Confirm

The Design partner asks before it proposes. It turns what the document doesn't settle into a short list of questions, at most six, ordered by how much they affect the structure. Each question points to the passage that prompted it, and every one can be skipped. For STAT 110, for example:

- The schedule has no topic in week 8. Is that exam week, a break, or something else?
- Enrolment isn't stated. Roughly how many students?
- The project is 40% with one due date. Do you want milestones with feedback, or is the single deadline deliberate?
- An outcome isn't assessed by anything. Assess it, is it inside another task, or drop it?

The Design partner **asks instead of guessing**. It never silently corrects your weights, dates, or wording. There is also one open question: in a sentence or two, how do you like to teach this course, and what do you want from this redesign? Your answer shapes the options in the next stage.

**Confirm your outcomes.** Before any structure is proposed, you confirm the outcomes the course will be built on:

- Your words stay as written. Where the Design partner has a better-worded alternative, it appears as a labelled suggestion with its reason. Choose **Use this**, **Edit**, or **Keep mine**.
- If the syllabus lists no outcomes, or you ask for help, the Design partner can suggest three to six observable outcomes drawn from the schedule and assessments. Each one is a labelled draft with a reason, and only your click confirms it: **Use this**, **Edit**, or **Skip**. You can also write your own.
- The record keeps track of which outcomes came from the syllabus, which you wrote, and which you accepted from a suggestion.

The next stage opens only after you confirm the outcomes.

### 4. Approaches

The Design partner proposes **three ways to structure the course**, each as a card. One is always the option closest to your syllabus. For STAT 110 the three were a weekly cadence, project-driven units built around the 40% project, and two-week cases that each test a claim. Every card shows:

- What it would do, and why it **fits**, with citations to your syllabus and quotes from your answers.
- What it **changes** compared with your syllabus.
- The **trade-offs**, stated plainly.
- A one-line evidence note.
- The number of modules and the expected average and peak weekly hours against your budget.

**Overlays** apply to any approach:

- **Bookends** add a Start here module and a closing module.
- **Spaced review** brings each module's check back in later modules.
- **UDL choice points** let students choose how they show their work at set points.
- **Teaching-presence plan** adds module overviews and facilitation prompts. It's optional for in-person courses and recommended for online ones.

Pick one approach, or combine several. If you combine them, the Design partner explains how they fit together before you go on.

Finally, write one sentence on why the choice fits your students. It's required, and it's saved to your design record.

### 5. Preview

The preview is the exact change set: what will be created, module by module, before anything is. It opens with a summary line, for example:

> Will add 7 modules, 16 lessons, 16 checks, 5 assignments, 6 outcomes and 24 alignment links. Renames nothing. Removes nothing.

The preview also shows:

- **A row per module.** Its objective, the outcomes it serves, its lessons, its assessment, where in the syllabus it came from, and its estimated hours.
- **Existing content.** Your existing modules and lessons stay exactly where they are, and new modules follow them. Where a new module overlaps one of yours by topic, the preview marks it.
- **Readings.** Readings with a source in your syllabus are linked. Where the syllabus doesn't give a reading, you see a placeholder such as "[Article to select]" and how many there are.
- **Dates and weights.** Where the Design partner had to place something the syllabus doesn't state, such as a due date, the preview says how it decided. Due dates fall on your meeting days and never in a week you said was an exam week or a break. If the syllabus doesn't give an assessment's points or weight, the preview asks you to enter them, with the passage it looked at, before you can apply. Any assessment that replaces one of yours is flagged so you can confirm its weight.
- **Readiness forecast.** How the course is expected to fare against your institution's course template and readiness checks after it's applied, and how many drafts will still need your review.
- **Which module are you least sure about?** Optional. The Design partner flags it for extra review and drafts two alternative openings for its first lesson.

Each preview has a plan ID. If the course changes after you preview, Tessera tells you and asks you to preview again, so what's applied is always what you saw.

Choose **Apply as drafts**. Creation runs in the background, usually about two minutes, and you can leave the page. Each lesson is written whole or not at all, so a failure never leaves a half-written lesson. If you want a different structure, choose **Change approach**.

### 6. Review

Applying opens the course workspace, where you review module by module.

**What gets created.** All of it as drafts:

- **Outcomes** you confirmed, added after any the course already has. Existing outcomes are never replaced.
- **Modules** with objectives, and **lessons** with their own objectives and time estimates.
- **Draft assignments** written as **Purpose, Task, and Criteria**, with a rubric drafted from the module objective and outcomes.
- **Alignment links** from each check, scenario, and assignment to the outcomes it assesses.
- **Starter content** in each lesson: a heading, a short prompt or callout, text, and a check with options and feedback. It's deliberately unfinished. Text blocks contain **[Your …] slots**, such as "[Your worked example]", for the one thing only you can write.
- **Start here** lesson, if you chose the bookends overlay, with navigation text, your contact details from the syllabus, the outcomes, an AI-use disclosure, and a short baseline check.

**The review panel.** Beside each module, the Design partner shows:

- **Why this module**, with the objective and the syllabus passages that led to it.
- **Why this block** on each block.
- **Next for this module**, a short list of the steps that are yours to take, such as choosing a reading where it left a placeholder, adding your own example to a slot, moving one of your existing lessons into a new module (it stays yours, unchanged), or confirming an assessment weight.
- **Add with AI** quick actions for a scenario, a worked example, a 15-minute variant, or a plain-language variant. Each one lands as a draft too.
- A **publish readiness** summary covering alignment, accessibility, time, the Start here lesson, and how much of the AI's work a person has kept.

On each block you choose **Keep**, **Edit**, **Revert** (when there's earlier content), or **Regenerate**, exactly as with other [AI drafts](/product/authoring). Your design record, including your answers and choices, can be exported as JSON, or as a CSV course map.

## Safety and trust

- **Everything is a draft until you keep it.** Every block the Design partner creates is labelled a draft and needs a person to keep it. A lesson can't be published while it still has unreviewed AI drafts.
- **Nothing is published automatically.** Starting from a syllabus never changes what learners see.
- **Nothing existing is renamed or removed.** The preview says so before you apply, and new modules are added after yours.
- **One action undoes the plan.** **Undo plan** removes what the Design partner added and you haven't touched. Anything you kept or edited stays, and Tessera lists what it kept and why. Your own lessons are never removed.
- **You see the evidence.** Every claim cites the page or section it came from, and the reasoning is one click away on each module and block.
- **The scope is narrow.** Only the syllabus is read. Student names are removed before analysis, and nothing is used to train a model.

## For administrators

The Design partner is controlled from the AI policy on the admin Policy page. Instructors see the policy on the Start page.

- **On or off.** Turn the Design partner on or off for the institution. It is on by default where AI authoring is on.
- **Allowed course structures.** Choose which approaches the Design partner may propose. If you allow only some, it offers only those.
- **Workload rates.** The workload estimate uses editable rates: pages of textbook reading per hour, hours per problem set, hours per page of writing, hours for a project, minutes per quiz, and minutes per discussion. Tessera starts you with default values. An instructor can adjust the assumptions for one course from the Read stage, and your policy remains the default for everyone else.
- **Default AI-use disclosure.** Set the statement that's drafted into each course's Start here lesson. Instructors can edit it, and because it's an AI draft like any other, they must keep it before publishing.

Institution and program [templates](/product/templates) apply as usual: the preview shows which template requirements the new structure meets, and the [readiness](/product/readiness) checks run on the result.

## FAQ

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="How long does reading take?">
    Usually one to three minutes, depending on the length of the document. It runs in the background, so you can leave the page and come back. Applying the preview also runs in the background, usually in about two minutes.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What file types can I use?">
    PDF and DOCX, up to 25 MB. Scanned PDFs are read with OCR first. You can also choose a file already in the course, or paste text, including a training brief or competency list. Word 97–2003 (`.doc`) files must be saved as `.docx` or PDF first.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What if my syllabus has no weekly schedule?">
    The read marks the schedule as missing rather than guessing, and asks for what it needs, such as the length of the term. Modules are laid out across the term length the syllabus states or that you confirm, and the approaches rest on your outcomes, assessments, and answers. Estimates that depend on a weekly schedule are less precise.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What if my syllabus lists no outcomes?">
    The Design partner can suggest three to six observable outcomes from your schedule and assessments, each with its reason. You choose **Use this**, **Edit**, or **Skip** for each, or write your own. Nothing is confirmed until you click.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What if the document is very long?">
    The Design partner reads up to a set length in one pass. If your document is longer, it tells you which pages it read.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can I skip the questions?">
    Yes. Every question can be skipped. Confirming your outcomes is the one required step before the approaches appear.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Will it invent readings or links?">
    No. Readings come only from your syllabus. Where a reading is missing, you get a placeholder such as "[Article to select]". A link appears only when the syllabus gives it.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Do students see any of this?">
    No. Students see the published course. Content that started as an AI draft and that you kept shows the usual "Drafted with AI · edited by" line.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
