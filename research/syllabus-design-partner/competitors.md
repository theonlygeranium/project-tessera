# Competitive landscape: AI course-building / "syllabus-to-course" features (as of late September 2026)

Purpose: inform the design of Tessera's "AI instructional design partner" (syllabus upload -> deep instructional analysis -> multiple structural proposals -> faculty pick/combine -> provisioned modules with starter content for review). Everything below is sourced; items marked **[unverified]** could not be confirmed against a primary source during this pass. Fetch failures are noted where relevant.

Research method: web search + vendor docs, release notes, campus CTL/LMS support pages, EDUCAUSE/Tyton reports, Inside Higher Ed, practitioner blogs. Two sources could not be read: a Springer *Discover Education* article on "accelerators and multipliers" in AI-assisted course design (rate-limited) and NPR's Oct 2025 piece on professors' AI use (robots.txt). Reddit threads did not surface in search results with enough specificity to cite; faculty sentiment is instead drawn from Inside Higher Ed, practitioner Substacks, and campus support pages.

## 1. Competitor-by-competitor capability profiles

### 1.1 Instructure Canvas: IgniteAI / IgniteAI Agent

**What exists (Sept 2026).**
- **IgniteAI Agent** launched March 12, 2026 (US + LatAm first), powered by Anthropic Claude via Amazon Bedrock. Instructure describes it as an "action-oriented assistant that can complete complex workflows across Canvas using a single prompt," backed by 500+ Canvas APIs. Named workflows: create and organize course modules, adjust due dates, generate rubrics, "align content to course materials," accessibility cleanup, review discussions, and "seek complementary content matched to existing materials." Free through June 30, 2026 (US) / Sept 30, 2026 (global), then a paid add-on. [Instructure press release, Mar 2026](https://www.instructure.com/press-release/instructure-delivers-its-agentic-ai-promise-launch-igniteai-agent); [Community feature overview](https://community.instructure.com/en/discussion/664514/feature-overview-igniteai-agent-for-canvas); [Inside Higher Ed, Mar 23 2026](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/tech-innovation/artificial-intelligence/2026/03/23/canvas-unrolls-ai-teaching-agent).
- **Point-tools (the older "IgniteAI" suite, GA since 2025):** rubric generator from an assignment, SpeedGrader grading assistance, discussion insights/summaries/translations, quiz question authoring from course materials (New Quizzes only), Smart Search. All disabled by default; several are paid add-ons. [U. Delaware Canvas@UD, May 5 2026](https://sites.udel.edu/canvas/2026/05/canvas-igniteai-tools/).
- **Block Content Editor** with "AI-powered suggestions for content and layout" (Canvas Plus/Next tiers) — early adopter program Sept 2026. [Instructure July 2026 product updates](https://www.instructure.com/resources/blog/new-tools-elevate-teaching-learning-and-student-success-july-2026-product-updates).
- **Khanmigo Teacher Tools** inside Canvas (since 2024/2025): discussion prompts, rubrics, exit tickets, lesson-plan style tools "without needing to write AI prompts." [UT Arlington AI resources](https://ai.uta.edu/category/resources); [Instructure press release](https://www.instructure.com/press-release/instructure-announces-release-ai-features-k-12-and-higher-ed-us-access-khanmigo).

**Inputs.** Natural-language prompts; a community prompt library and a "Prompt Builder." The agent reads *existing course content* through APIs (e.g., "generate two discussion prompts based on the Module 3 Overview page"; quiz questions from an uploaded PDF teaching guide). No documented "upload your syllabus and build the course" flow; campus guides note users paste outcomes into prompts. [Xavier U. guide](https://www.xavier.edu/teachingwithtech/a-z/ignite-ai); [AWS Public Sector blog, Jul 24 2025](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/publicsector/redefining-instructional-support-with-generative-ai-how-instructure-built-igniteai-on-aws/); [Getting started KB](https://community.instructure.com/en/kb/articles/664307-how-do-i-get-started-using-the-igniteai-agent).

**Outputs.** Modules (creation/organization), assignments, quizzes, rubrics, discussion prompts, announcements, outcomes/performance assessments; also an "alignment review" use case (which outcomes lack assessment). [Xavier U. guide](https://www.xavier.edu/teachingwithtech/a-z/ignite-ai).

**Human review.** Agent "asks before completing any action" and previews planned steps; Instructure acknowledged it is still building "a warning that will double confirm with users before any data in their course is overwritten." Users reported "Try Again" errors, and there is no assignment revision history for recovery. [Community feature overview](https://community.instructure.com/en/discussion/664514/feature-overview-igniteai-agent-for-canvas).

**Pedagogy grounding.** None explicit. Positioned as reducing "low-value" admin tasks; the "design partner" language is marketing, not a framework. [Inside Higher Ed, Mar 2026](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/tech-innovation/artificial-intelligence/2026/03/23/canvas-unrolls-ai-teaching-agent).

**Criticism.** Jason Gulya (Berkeley College): concern for student–teacher relationship when rubrics/messages are AI-made, and that institutions will use time savings to justify heavier loads, risking "a classroom mostly void of human interaction." [Inside Higher Ed, Mar 2026](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/tech-innovation/artificial-intelligence/2026/03/23/canvas-unrolls-ai-teaching-agent). Overwrite/deletion risk and inconsistent reliability from the beta community thread above.

### 1.2 Blackboard Learn Ultra: AI Design Assistant (Anthology -> "new Blackboard")

**Corporate status (verified).** Anthology missed a debt payment Dec 2024, entered Chapter 11 in Sept 2025, sold enterprise ERP/CRM to **Ellucian** (~$70M, closed end-Dec 2025) and lifecycle/student-success products to **Encoura** (~$50M, closed end-Jan 2026), and emerged Feb 27, 2026 as a standalone **Blackboard** owned by Nexus/Oaktree with ~$70M new financing; co-founder Matt Pittinsky returning as CEO by Oct 2026. [Phil Hill, "The New Blackboard Emerges"](https://onedtech.philhillaa.com/p/the-new-blackboard-emerges-from-bankruptcy); [GovTech on Ellucian deal](https://www.govtech.com/education/higher-ed/ellucian-acquires-anthologys-erp-sis); [Inside Higher Ed, Oct 8 2025](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/tech-innovation/administrative-tech/2025/10/08/why-ed-tech-behemoth-unraveled). Hill characterizes the runway as "meaningful but not transformative" — a signal that AI roadmap investment is constrained.

**Capabilities (help doc last modified Sept 10, 2026).** Learning-module (course structure) suggestions; module images; test questions and question banks; "authentic assignments"; rubrics; discussion/journal prompts; AI Conversation (student-vs-AI-persona); document images; Unsplash keyword suggestions; **Context Picker** ("uses the course context you choose"); language selector. Admins enable each feature individually; metadata tracks auto-generated vs. edited content. [Anthology admin help](https://help.anthology.com/blackboard/administrator/en/tools-management/blackboard-ai-design-assistant.html).

**Inputs.** Course title + optional pasted context ("learning objectives, course outline, or syllabus") + Context Picker to select existing course files/Ultra Documents. Ten complexity levels (Level 1 "early primary" to Level 10 "advanced PhD"). No explicit Bloom's selector for module generation. [NIU Blackboard guide](https://www.niu.edu/blackboard/content/ai-design-assistant.shtml); [FHSU TILT FAQ](https://tigerlearn.fhsu.edu/ai-design-assistant-in-blackboard-ultra-faq/). A separate **AI Design Assistant for Documents** (July 2025) analyzes an existing document and recommends layouts, visuals, and formative checks. [Anthology July 15 2025 announcement](https://www.blackboard.com/news/anthology-unveils-next-generation-ai-and-immersive-learning-innovations-in-blackboard).

**Outputs.** *Module titles and descriptions* (a structural skeleton), not populated modules; plus the item-level generators above. Southampton's digital-learning team explicitly frames the structure generator as "a brainstorming partner, not a content creator." [U. Southampton, May 2025](https://generic.wordpress.soton.ac.uk/digital-learning/2025/05/12/structure-generator).

**Human review.** All output is suggestion-level; instructor must review before publish; institutions layer their own QA heuristics (Southampton's "PREP"/"EDIT"). Ally integration: Anthology's admin page does not list an Ally alt-text feature under AI Design Assistant **[unverified as a distinct AI Design Assistant feature]**.

**Pedagogy grounding.** Marketing language about "authentic assessment" (apply/analyze/evaluate/create) but no named ID framework. [FHSU FAQ](https://tigerlearn.fhsu.edu/ai-design-assistant-in-blackboard-ultra-faq/).

**Criticism.** Campus FAQs concede output "depends on the quality and clarity of the input," bias "particularly apparent when generating images," and that faculty must fact-check every test item. [NIU](https://www.niu.edu/blackboard/content/ai-design-assistant.shtml); [FHSU](https://tigerlearn.fhsu.edu/ai-design-assistant-in-blackboard-ultra-faq/). Marc Watkins (Univ. of Mississippi) raised the foundational objections when the feature first shipped: "If I let an algorithm design my curriculum...what does that say to administration about my labor?" and the hypocrisy problem ("How can we expect students not to use AI...if we're using it to develop learning materials?"). [Watkins, Sept 2023](https://marcwatkins.substack.com/p/automation-arrives-in-the-classroom). 2025 release notes show AI investment shifting toward grading/feedback (rubric summaries, feedback rewriting) rather than deeper course design. [2025 archived release notes](https://help.anthology.com/blackboard/administrator/en/whats-new/2025-archived-release-notes.html).

### 1.3 D2L Brightspace: Lumi (Lumi Pro paid tier)

**Capabilities.** Lumi is a set of point features inside existing Brightspace tools, built on Anthropic Claude "using content provided by the course creator": practice questions in HTML topics; quiz question generation + question banks + AI feedback (Lumi Quiz); discussion/assignment ideas (Lumi Idea/Ideas); module summaries/overviews (Lumi Summary); **Lumi Remix** (transform an existing PowerPoint/materials into a Creator+ module, retone, translate); Lumi Chat and Lumi Tutor (student-facing, June 2026); Lumi Insights with suggested interventions; Lumi Learner Mode (beta fall 2026). [Stony Brook Lumi page](https://it.stonybrook.edu/services/brightspace/lumi); [D2L What's New H1 2026](https://www.d2l.com/whats-new/); [D2L Fusion 2026 recap](https://www.d2l.com/blog/in-case-you-missed-it-product-highlights-from-fusion-2026/); [D2L press release, Jul 9 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/d2l-announces-new-ai-enhanced-content-creation-and-personalized-learning-at-fusion-2026-302822234.html).

**Inputs.** Existing course content selected by the instructor; Remix takes an uploaded deck/document. No documented syllabus-to-course-structure generator as of H1 2026. [D2L What's New](https://www.d2l.com/whats-new/).

**Outputs.** Item-level artifacts and remixed modules; no multi-module structure proposal.

**Human review.** Output lands in the editor for review; SUNY's contract requires instructors to disclose AI-generated content in syllabi. [Stony Brook](https://it.stonybrook.edu/services/brightspace/lumi).

**Adoption/market signal.** Lumi ARR only ~$3.5M of D2L's $219.8M (<2%) at FYE Jan 2026; >40% attach among new higher-ed customers; "AI functionality is not yet showing up consistently in RFPs"; D2L frames AI as "a method to scale course creation and redesign" with an unverifiable 50% time-savings claim. [Phil Hill, D2L Fusion 2026 notes](https://onedtech.philhillaa.com/p/d2l-fusion-conference-notes-2026).

### 1.4 Moodle (core 5.x AI subsystem) + partners

**Core.** Moodle 5.x AI subsystem provides *placements* only: text editor (generate text, generate image) and course assistance (summarise, explain). All disabled by default; from 5.1 teachers can toggle per course/activity. **No core course-structure or module generation.** [MoodleDocs AI placements 5.1](https://docs.moodle.org/501/en/AI_placements); [AI providers 5.2](https://docs.moodle.org/502/en/AI_providers).

**Plugins/partners filling the gap.**
- *Course Creator AI* (local_coursegen, Datacurso): accepts a syllabus document and an instructional-design-model selection; generates sections and activities. Very small footprint (3 active sites, 66 downloads as of the listing; approval review opened Oct 2025). [moodle.org plugin page](https://moodle.org/plugins/local_coursegen).
- *Edwiser Smart AI Course Creator*: prompt or PDF/DOCX/TXT upload -> sections/lessons, 29 H5P types, quizzes, SCORM or API push to Moodle; credit-based pricing from $29/mo (page updated Jul 23 2026). [Edwiser](https://edwiser.org/smart-ai-course-creator-for-moodle/).
- Question-bank generators (qbank_genai, local_aiquestions, qbank_questiongen, block_openai_questions) all positioned as "a first draft a human must review." [edzlms roundup, 2026](https://edzlms.com/ai-plugins-for-moodle-2026/).
- OpenLMS-specific AI authoring: **[unverified]** — nothing distinct surfaced beyond the Moodle ecosystem above.

### 1.5 Coursera Course Builder / edX-2U

**Coursera Course Builder.** Launched 2023 for enterprise; expanded to educator partners Sept 2025. Inputs: outline/description/objectives typed by the author, "course ingestion of existing materials" via Common Cartridge, or "authoring from scratch with just a course idea." Outputs: outline, descriptions, learning objectives, AI-generated assessment questions (editable; AI-graded essay/code/video with auto rubrics), and recommended Coursera catalog content to blend in. Includes "Instructional Design Guidance from an AI-powered coach." Coursera claims >4,000 enterprise courses and an 87% median time reduction; campus pilot: 88% of authors satisfied. [Coursera blog, AI innovations](https://blog.coursera.org/new-ai-powered-innovations-on-coursera); [Coursera for Campus Course Builder page](https://www.coursera.org/campus/course-builder); [Original launch post](https://blog.coursera.org/coursera-launches-course-builder). Note this is catalog-blending, not a syllabus-analysis workflow.

**edX / 2U.** Public AI announcements center on ChatGPT-based *learner* assistants (Xpert) and program marketing; no instructor-facing course-generation tool surfaced. **[unverified: whether Open edX Studio has shipped generative authoring]**. [2U newsroom](https://2u.com/newsroom/edx-debuts-two-ai-powered-learning-assistants-built-on-chatgpt/).

**ASU "Atomic" (cautionary case).** ASU soft-launched a $5/month Claude-based builder that stitches faculty lecture videos/readings from Canvas into personalized modules. Faculty found their lectures reused without notice ("Frankensteinian"), factual errors ("Client Brooks"), and warned that out-of-context clips could be weaponized against instructors teaching sensitive topics; ASU's president called it "not really ready for prime time." [Inside Higher Ed, Apr 29 2026](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/tech-innovation/artificial-intelligence/2026/04/29/faculty-concerned-about-asus-new-ai-course); [EdScoop](https://edscoop.com/faculty-at-asu-are-concerned-about-a-new-ai-powered-course-builder/). Lesson: provenance, consent, and context preservation are first-order faculty concerns for any tool that ingests their materials.

### 1.7 Articulate Rise 360: AI Assistant / AI Course Drafts

**Inputs.** Free-text description + drag-and-drop source documents, pasted text, or URLs; adjustable topic, tone, audience, goals; editable learning objectives; duration (single lesson to extended); interactive vs. text-only. **Outputs.** Outline (overview + lesson titles), lessons with blocks chosen "following best practices from the Rise Business Content library," banner + 2–3 images per lesson, optional final quiz; export outline as PDF/Word for stakeholder review. Q1 2026 added AI images in drafts, duration control, Magic Import (storyboards -> lessons/blocks/quizzes), image extraction from PDFs/slides. [Articulate support: AI Course Drafts (updated Sept 14 2026)](https://www.articulatesupport.com/article/AI-Assistant-in-Rise-360-AI-Course-Drafts); [Articulate Q1 2026 release blog](https://www.articulate.com/blog/articulate-feature-release-whats-new-in-q1-2026/).

**Human review.** Edit-as-it-generates; sparkle-icon AI edits; no staged approval gates.

**Criticism.** Maestro's review: content "lacks depth," oversimplifies specialized topics, "doesn't use Bloom's Taxonomy or other learning models," inconsistent tone, weak images, no automatic alt text, and fact-checking burden "undermine[s] promised efficiency gains." [Maestro review](https://maestrolearning.com/blogs/articulate-rise-360-ai/).

## 2. Cross-cutting patterns

### What (almost) everyone does
1. **One-shot generation from a prompt box or a single document**: Rise, 360Learning, Sana, Absorb, Coursebox, Edwiser, LearnWorlds, Thinkific, Coursera. The LMS incumbents (Canvas, Blackboard, D2L) instead ship *per-item* generate buttons (rubric, quiz, discussion, module titles) inside existing editors.
2. **"Structure = titles":** Blackboard's module generator and Thinkific's outline generator produce headings, not designed modules. [Southampton](https://generic.wordpress.soton.ac.uk/digital-learning/2025/05/12/structure-generator); [Thinkific](https://support.thinkific.com/hc/en-us/articles/16321227901335-AI-Course-Outline-Generator).
3. **Single proposal, then edit.** Every tool returns one outline; the affordance for alternatives is "regenerate."
4. **Review = "it's editable."** Only Absorb (outline approval gate) and Docebo (outline-stage inspection) describe a staged checkpoint; Canvas's agent confirms *actions* but not *design*. [Absorb](https://support.absorblms.com/hc/en-us/articles/52047105413395-Introducing-Aura-Create-The-AI-Powered-Course-Authoring-Agent); [Docebo](https://www.docebo.com/learning-network/blog/docebo-ai/).
5. **Homogeneous outputs.** Philippa Hardman tested four frontier models on the same brief: all converged on categorization drag-drops, single-turn MCQs, click-to-reveal, and a summative check, "regardless of actual performance barriers," with silent omissions and confidently invented metrics (pass marks, seat times). [Hardman, "AI Can Build Your Course, but Can it Design the Learning?"](https://drphilippahardman.substack.com/p/ai-can-build-your-course-but-can).
6. **Complexity dials as the pedagogy surface.** Blackboard's 10 levels, LearnWorlds' "knowledge level," Rise's tone/duration — proxies for audience, not design rationale.
7. **Grounding on existing content is the 2026 differentiator among incumbents** (Canvas agent via APIs, D2L "98% of sessions cite course material," Blackboard Context Picker, Top Hat) — but grounding is used for *item generation*, not for *analysis of the course as a whole*.

### What almost nobody does
- **Multiple structural alternatives** (e.g., chronological vs. thematic vs. problem-based vs. backward-designed-from-summative) presented side by side with trade-offs. None found.
- **Alignment analysis** (course outcomes -> module outcomes -> activities -> assessments) surfaced as a *diagnostic*. A Canvas Community thread asking for exactly this sits unanswered; Xavier lists "identify which learning outcomes lack assessment" as a *prompt* one can try with IgniteAI Agent, not a product feature. Coursetune does this manually/visually without AI. [Canvas Community thread](https://community.instructure.com/en/discussion/619513/ai-canvas-and-course-alignment); [Xavier](https://www.xavier.edu/teachingwithtech/a-z/ignite-ai); [Coursetune](https://authoritypresswire.com/coursetune-launches-new-course-planning-software-to-help-instructors-design-quality-learning-through-visualizations-and-curriculum-alignment/).
- **Workload / time-on-task analysis** of the proposed design (reading load, assessment density, due-date clustering). Canvas can *shift* due dates; nobody *estimates* load. None found.
- **Explaining pedagogical rationale** for a given structure ("we sequenced X before Y because..."). Microsoft's Unit Plan includes essential questions; Coursera mentions an "instructional design coach"; neither exposes reasoning per design decision. [Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/education/blog/2026/06/ai-in-education-is-changing-fast-new-microsoft-365-education-experiences-put-learning-first/); [Coursera](https://blog.coursera.org/new-ai-powered-innovations-on-coursera).
- **Provenance tracking** at the artifact level (which syllabus passage, which faculty source, which AI generation produced this block). Blackboard tracks "auto-generated vs. edited" metadata; Blackboard AVA links answers to source; nobody exposes provenance to reviewers as a review affordance. The ASU case shows the cost of not doing so. [Anthology admin help](https://help.anthology.com/blackboard/administrator/en/tools-management/blackboard-ai-design-assistant.html); [Inside Higher Ed on ASU](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/tech-innovation/artificial-intelligence/2026/04/29/faculty-concerned-about-asus-new-ai-course).
- **Staged review with approval gates by design layer** (structure -> outcomes -> assessments -> content). Absorb is the only one with even a single gate. 
- **Learning the instructor's teaching philosophy / voice** and applying it across generations. Canvas can draft "in institutional tone"; LearnWorlds asks tone once per form. No persistent instructor profile found. [AWS blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/publicsector/redefining-instructional-support-with-generative-ai-how-instructure-built-igniteai-on-aws/).
- **Pulling institutional policies** (accessibility standards, credit-hour/workload norms, AI-use policy, QM/OSCQR rubrics) into generation constraints. Only Microsoft (academic standards) and ibl.ai (accreditation mapping, self-reported) gesture at this.
- **Needs/diagnostic analysis before generation.** Hardman: AI does "design craft" but structurally cannot do "design judgment" (is training the answer; which intervention). No LMS tool asks diagnostic questions before generating. [Hardman](https://drphilippahardman.substack.com/p/ai-can-build-your-course-but-can).

## 3. Faculty and ID-community criticism

**"Infodump, not a learning experience."** Devlin Peck (Jul 30 2026): "Nearly every learning vendor now ships a 'generate a course from this document' button. I have tried them. The output is structurally fine and instructionally hollow: no real scenario, no real practice, no point of view." [Devlin Peck, AI in ID 2026](https://www.devlinpeck.com/content/ai-in-instructional-design).

**Generic, framework-free output.** Rise "doesn't use Bloom's Taxonomy or other learning models"; fact-checking erodes time savings; missing alt text creates accessibility debt. [Maestro](https://maestrolearning.com/blogs/articulate-rise-360-ai/). Hardman: "strategic monoculture," "AI voice" bleeding into learner copy, "personalization without substance." [Hardman](https://drphilippahardman.substack.com/p/ai-can-build-your-course-but-can).

**Deskilling and labor politics.** Watkins: delegating curriculum to an algorithm signals to administration that the labor is automatable; features arrive via system update without training or choice. [Watkins](https://marcwatkins.substack.com/p/automation-arrives-in-the-classroom). Gulya: time savings become workload increases. ## 4. What "good" looks like, per practitioners and associations

- **Diagnose before you design.** Hardman's ideal workflow: (1) needs analysis producing an evidence-based problem statement; (2) decide intervention type (or that training isn't the answer); (3) SME + AI execute against a detailed brief (audience, constraints, strategy, success measures); (4) expert review for pedagogical fidelity *and* AI-specific error patterns (silent omissions, invented numbers, AI voice). [Hardman](https://drphilippahardman.substack.com/p/ai-can-build-your-course-but-can).
- **Design thinking over drafting.** Peck: use AI for drafts; keep human judgment over scenario logic, practice, tone, facts. [Peck](https://www.devlinpeck.com/content/ai-in-instructional-design).
- **Quality Matters + iDesign (Aug 26 2025 initiative; Feb 12 2026 EDUCAUSE Review piece by Deb Adair and Whitney Kilgore):** AI should "support, not supplant, human-centered design"; align tools with explicit instructional objectives; connect to established frameworks like QM; embed quality checks *during* design rather than post-hoc review; give faculty time and space to experiment; 84% of students still prefer help from people. [QM/iDesign press release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/online-learning-nonprofit-launches-new-initiative-to-explore-ais-role-in-the-future-of-instructional-design-and-quality-assurance-302536769.html); [Adair & Kilgore, EDUCAUSE Review](https://er.educause.edu/articles/2026/2/ai-and-course-design-machines-can-help-but-only-humans-can-teach).
- **OLC "AI Course Compass" (Marcus Green, 2025):** seven phases from institutional vision through curriculum mapping, ethical alignment, outcome-aligned tool selection, pilot, scale, evaluate; principles: systemic ethics checks, human oversight, faculty pedagogical authority, role clarity for IDs/faculty/admins. [OLC](https://onlinelearningconsortium.org/olc-insights/2025/05/ai-course-compass/).
- **Campus QA heuristics:** Southampton's PREP (Plan, Reflect, Evaluate, Publish) and EDIT (Evaluate, Determine, Improve, Tailor), with bias/accuracy/accessibility as review lenses. [Southampton](https://generic.wordpress.soton.ac.uk/digital-learning/2025/05/12/structure-generator).
- **Transparency and provenance as norms:** SUNY disclosure requirement; Blackboard's generated-vs-edited metadata; AVA's "linked to the original source." [Stony Brook](https://it.stonybrook.edu/services/brightspace/lumi); [Anthology Jul 2025](https://www.blackboard.com/news/anthology-unveils-next-generation-ai-and-immersive-learning-innovations-in-blackboard).
- **EDUCAUSE 2026 Top 10 #2, "The Human Edge of AI":** frames the institutional priority as augmenting rather than replacing human judgment. [EDUCAUSE Review](https://er.educause.edu/articles/2025/10/2026-educause-top-10-2-the-human-edge-of-ai). AECT/ATD-specific AI design principle statements were not located in this pass **[unverified]**.

## 6. Implications for Tessera (brief)

1. Lead with **analysis, not generation**: an "instructional read" of the syllabus (outcome quality, assessment map, sequencing logic, load, policy fit) is something no LMS does and directly answers the "infodump" critique.
2. **Offer 2–4 named architectures** (e.g., thematic, chronological, problem/case-based, backward-designed, competency-gated) each with rationale and trade-offs, and let faculty combine. This is the single clearest differentiator; nobody does it.
3. **Gate by layer** (structure -> outcomes -> assessments -> content), with diffs and undo; Canvas's overwrite incidents and the lack of revision history are a live pain point.
4. **Provenance and consent by default**: show which syllabus passage/faculty source each artifact came from; never ingest faculty materials outside the course without explicit opt-in (ASU lesson).
5. **Pull institutional constraints** (QM/OSCQR, credit-hour workload norms, accessibility standards, AI-use policy) into generation and surface compliance as part of review.
6. **Persistent instructor profile** (philosophy, voice, assessment preferences, AI-disclosure stance) applied across generations, addressing the "AI voice"/genericness critique.
7. Expect **skeptical buyers**: AI is <2% of D2L revenue and "not consistently in RFPs"; faculty policy-confidence is 22%. Position as a co-designer with visible reasoning, not a button.
